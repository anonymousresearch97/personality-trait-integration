Personality Trait Organization Across Adolescence and Adulthood
Point 5: completed checks and proposed revision wording
10 November 2026

Decision
Use the title above. Close the planned measurement work with the fixed checks
below and explicit limits on interpretation. No further item deletion,
model modification or additional invariance methods are proposed. This
addresses the reviewer request; it does not establish complete measurement
equivalence. The actual manuscript source has not been edited: the text and
replacement figures in this folder are ready for insertion.

Results to retain
1. Primary age-specific reliability: Cronbach's alpha=.784–.887 for the six
   16-item domains across ages 13–19, 20–39, 40–64 and 65–100 (N=98,819).
   These coefficients describe internal consistency, not age invariance.
2. A single fixed hierarchical model per domain used 16 items, four named
   facets and their higher-order domain. All 36 fits converged and had
   admissible variance/covariance estimates. Both loading levels were tested
   for equality across age groups. No post hoc modifications were made.

   H and A met the fixed screening criteria in every age group (robust
   CFI>=.90, robust RMSEA<=.08, SRMR<=.08). E was borderline, with adolescent
   CFI=.899727; it is not counted as passing by rounding. X, C and O did not
   meet these criteria in every group. These thresholds are descriptive
   screens, not universal evidence of acceptable measurement.
   All metric changes were small: CFI decreased .0011–.0031, RMSEA decreased
   .0016–.0033, and SRMR increased .0014–.0025. Nevertheless, robust nested
   chi-square tests rejected exact equality for all six domains (45 df,
   all p<2.2e−16). Small changes cannot compensate for poor baseline fit.
   Full six-domain measurement invariance is therefore not established.
3. SAPA: the raw reconstruction verified all 18,616 included participants and
   their existing scores. The variable item subsets often omit facets.
   Only 6.4%, 7.0%, 9.3% and 13.2% of respondents in the four successive
   age bands have even one answered item from every facet of all six domains.
   In the 60+ group (N=303), median item-pair overlap is only 7–14 respondents
   by domain. These are coverage diagnostics, not reliability estimates.
   A three-item minimum does not establish adequate domain coverage or
   comparable precision. SAPA remains a limited external comparison with
   different items and non-equivalent age categories.
4. Replacement figures show both standard Gaussian methods symmetrically,
   use undirected skeletons, define all six domains, and give actual ages
   and N. Primary groups: 13–19/7,738; 20–39/51,670; 40–64/35,496;
   65–100/3,915. SAPA bands: <=18/4,550; 19–39/11,510; 40–59/2,253;

Proposed concise main-text addition
Age-specific internal consistency in the primary sample ranged from .784 to
.887. Supplementary domain-wise measurement analyses found small changes in
fit when loadings were constrained across age groups, but baseline fit was
not adequate for all domains, and complete measurement invariance was not
established. We therefore interpret the networks as patterns among observed
HEXACO domain scores; measurement differences may contribute to apparent age
differences. SAPA provides a partial external comparison with non-equivalent
age bands and variable item and facet coverage, rather than validation of
the same developmental groups or a common lifespan trajectory.

Proposed supplementary Methods
We fitted one fixed hierarchical model for each primary HEXACO domain using
its 16 keyed items, four established facets and one higher-order domain.
Models were estimated separately within each age group and jointly across
the four groups using robust maximum likelihood (MLR; lavaan 0.7-3).
Configural models allowed age-specific parameters. Metric models equated
both item-to-facet and facet-to-domain loadings; marker loadings fixed factor
scales, while intercepts, residual variances, facet disturbances and domain
variances remained group-specific. Latent means were fixed at zero. We
reported robust CFI/TLI/RMSEA, SRMR, scaled chi-square and robust nested
model tests, with convergence and admissibility checks. The screening rules
were fixed before these fits and are documented in analysis_plan.txt.
Five-category responses were treated as numeric. These checks do not test
ordinal thresholds, scalar invariance, equality of composite reliability,
or the full six-domain cross-loading and discriminant structure. The models
were supplementary checks developed during revision, not preregistered
confirmatory analyses. No post hoc model adjustments were undertaken.

Proposed reviewer response (factual analysis statements; insert accompanying
text/tables into the manuscript before describing those insertions as made)
We agree that age-specific reliability and measurement comparability are
distinct requirements. We calculated primary age-specific internal
consistency (alpha=.784–.887) and fitted fixed domain-wise hierarchical
configural and metric models across the four adolescent/adult groups.
Equal-loading constraints at both hierarchical levels produced small changes
in fit (robust delta-CFI=−.0011 to −.0031). However, only Honesty–Humility
and Agreeableness met our baseline fit screen in every age group;
Emotionality was borderline and the remaining domains showed baseline
misfit. Exact equality was rejected in all six domains. We therefore do not
claim complete measurement invariance or that age differences in the
estimated networks necessarily reflect developmental reorganization of
latent traits. For SAPA, we verified scoring and quantified answered-item,
facet and item-pair coverage. The variable, frequently incomplete item
subsets do not establish comparable score reliability or construct coverage;
the SAPA findings require explicitly limited external-comparison claims.
The accompanying revision text states these limitations and reserves the
small childhood sample for exploratory supplementary reporting.
