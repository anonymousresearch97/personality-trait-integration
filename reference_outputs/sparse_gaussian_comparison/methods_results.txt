Stricter Gaussian comparison — 20 October 2026

Completed one fixed, user-requested sensitivity comparison: Gaussian
PC-stable with Fisher-z alpha=.01 (previously .05), and exact Gaussian BIC
with twice its usual complexity penalty. These settings were fixed before
the new results were inspected; no threshold sweep was performed.

The modified score maximizes sum_i[logL_i - lambda/2*(parents_i+2)*log(N)],
with lambda=2 instead of the ordinary BIC value 1. Parameters include the
intercept, slopes and residual variance. For six nodes, K=12+number of edges.
Report this as stronger-penalty Gaussian BIC, not ordinary BIC. All reported
links are undirected skeleton edges, maximum 15; selected DAG directions
are one optimizing representation and do not establish causal directions.

Interpretation
The stricter settings reduce the equal-N mean link counts in every group
for both methods. However, the largest full-sample groups remain dense:
primary early adulthood 14/15 and SAPA 19–39 at 13/15 under both methods.
Primary equal-N early adulthood retains the highest mean for both methods.
For BIC, its advantage over middle and older adulthood is only .246 and
.276 links, respectively. This is a small descriptive distinction.
SAPA equal-N means increase across the actual bands under both methods;
there is no early-band maximum. Thus parsimony improves, and the primary
mean maximum survives this sensitivity, but the SAPA pattern does not
replicate it. No universal developmental peak is established.

For primary PC, early adulthood is strictly highest in 51.0% of matched
draw combinations and highest including ties in 81.1%. BIC values are 26.9%
and 89.4%: 62.5% involve a tie for the maximum. These are descriptive
frequencies conditional on the observed source samples and sampling scheme,
not probabilities that a population peak exists and not significance tests.

Sampling and limitations
Scores, filters and age bands are unchanged. Draws are without replacement
within a draw and may overlap across draws. PC and BIC use identical people
per draw. Every row-selection hash matches the previous comparison; SAPA
participant-ID hashes also match. Equal N is within each dataset, not between
datasets: 3, 000 primary versus 300 SAPA. Primary childhood is only fitted in
full(N 98), not in the equal-N comparison. SAPA has no separable childhood
group. Its public age bands and personality instrument are not equivalent
to those in the primary dataset.
The saved 2.5–97.5 percentile ranges describe subsampling variability, not
population confidence intervals. SAPA oldest draws contain 300 of 303 people,
so their narrow variability is not evidence of high population precision.
Gaussian modeling assumptions remain. Lines in the plot connect descriptive
age-group estimates; they are not individual developmental trajectories.
