Continuous-score Gaussian sensitivity: interpretation

The original full-sample edge counts are 4,13,14,13,10 for Gaussian PC-stable
and 6,13,14,13,10 for exact Gaussian BIC, from childhood through older adulthood.
These graphs are much denser than the discretized networks. They use a
different model with linear Gaussian conditional distributions; the agreement
with an early-adulthood maximum is not validation of the original discrete
estimation or a reason by itself to choose the Gaussian specification.

At matched N = 3,000 over 1,000 draws per nonchildhood group:
                         PC mean(SD)          BIC mean(SD)
Adolescence              10.305(0.944)        10.157(0.913)
Early adulthood          10.762(1.057)        10.489(0.800)
Middle adulthood          9.093(0.937)         9.514(0.675)
Older adulthood           9.467(0.777)         9.374(0.516)

Early adulthood has the largest mean under both methods, but the advantage
over adolescence is small:0.457 edges for PC and 0.332 for BIC. Across paired
replicate indices, early-minus-adolescent counts range from -2 to 3 between
the empirical 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles under each method. Early adulthood is
strictly the largest of all four groups in 43.2% of PC draw comparisons and
32.8% of BIC comparisons; it is largest or tied in 70.6% and 70.8%, respectively.
These are descriptive probabilities over repeated draws from these observed
datasets, not population p-values, significance tests, or probabilities that
a developmental peak is real. They do not establish individual development.

The modest net difference is a balance of appreciable changes in link identity.
Early-adulthood versus adolescent selection frequencies increase for C-H
(PC:1.000 vs  0.026; BIC:1.000 vs  0.203), A-O(0.943 vs  0.479;0.854 vs  0.357),
O-X(0.986 vs  0.770;0.964 vs  0.707), and H-O(0.870 vs  0.605;0.930 vs  0.739).
They decrease strongly for C-O(0.259 vs  0.998;0.224 vs  0.967), A-E
(0.410 vs  0.693;0.189 vs  0.673), and E-O(0.263 vs  0.598;0.191 vs  0.550).
C-H is also selected in every middle- and older-adulthood draw, so that link
does not uniquely distinguish early adulthood. The results do not support a
claim that all or most relationships strengthen in early adulthood. Selection
frequencies measure algorithmic retention, not association magnitudes.

All 15 pair frequencies are reported, including absent and unstable links.
No model settings were searched for a desired age pattern. All 8000 skeleton
counts,4,000 DAG acyclicity checks, and matched-sample membership hashes passed.
Local Gaussian scores agree with 384 independent raw-data OLS calculations
within 1.37e-12;150 partial correlations agree with residualization within
3.34e-16; subset-DP scores agree with exhaustive enumeration of all 543 four-
node DAGs on both a childhood and a 3000-person sample. The rerun after replacing
BLAS arithmetic only in independent validation generated byte-identical fits.

Domain abbreviations: H=Honesty-Humility,E=Emotionality,X=Extraversion,
A=Agreeableness,C=Conscientiousness,O=Openness.
